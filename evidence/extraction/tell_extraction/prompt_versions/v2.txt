You are a behavioral analyst reviewing a completed game of Werewolf. Your job is to mine
candidate TELLS: recurring, publicly-observable behavior patterns that might turn out to
correlate with a player's hidden role. You see the true roles; the players did not.

GAME RULES:
{game_rules}

TRUE ROLES (post-game reveal — for your orientation only):
{roles_line}

PUBLIC RECORD OF EARLIER DAYS (context):
{prior_days}

FOCUS DAY {focus_day} — PUBLIC RECORD (mine THIS day only):
{day_record}

TASK:
List every distinct candidate tell exhibited on day {focus_day}. A candidate tell is a
behavior that (a) a player at the table could observe with no hidden information, (b) is
described generally enough that the same pattern could recur in a different game with
different players, and (c) is specific enough that a reader could check "did player X do
this?" against a transcript.

For each tell, emit:
- exhibitor: the player_id of who did it (IDs are allowed HERE and only here).
- behavior: ONE sentence, present tense, the general pattern. Rules:
  - Describe only what is observable in public discussion and votes. Never reference
    night actions, private reasoning, or information a player at the table lacks.
  - No player IDs. No role names (wolf, villager, healer, investigator, vigilante,
    serial killer) and no faction words (town, evil) — the behavior must be phrased so
    it could be checked by someone who does not know any roles.
  - No intent or hidden-state attribution ("to hide their guilt", "because they are
    nervous"). Describe the act, not the motive. Purpose clauses ("to establish
    credibility", "in order to deflect", "intended to discredit") are motive
    attribution — banned, UNLESS you are describing the content of what the player
    themselves said (e.g. "claims their earlier vote was meant to break a deadlock"
    is fine: the claim itself is the observable act).
  - Phrase at the grain of a MOVE TYPE — deflecting, counter-accusing, bandwagoning,
    volunteering information, going quiet — that many different players in many
    different games could exhibit. If your description needs this game's specific
    storyline to make sense, generalize it; if it cannot be generalized without
    becoming empty, skip it.
  - GOOD: "deflects onto their accuser rather than addressing the accusation when
    directly challenged"
  - GOOD: "votes for the player the room has already converged on without having
    voiced any suspicion of them during discussion"
  - GOOD: "announces having been the target of a failed night attack"
  - BAD: "acts suspiciously like a wolf" (role word; not checkable)
  - BAD: "player_3 attacks player_7" (IDs; not a general pattern)
  - BAD: "announces the failed attack on them to establish credibility" (motive
    attribution — describe the announcement, not its purpose)
  - BAD: "suggests a group activity where each player names the most cautious player"
    (this game's storyline re-described, not a reusable pattern)
- evidence_quote: ONE CONTIGUOUS verbatim span (at most 25 words) copied exactly from
  the focus day's record above — the moment that shows the behavior. Never splice
  separate sentences together and never use "..." to skip or trim words; if the
  behavior spans several utterances, quote the single clearest one.

WHAT TO SURFACE AND WHAT TO SKIP:
- Surface behaviors from ALL players regardless of their true role — patterns exhibited
  by villagers matter as much as patterns exhibited by wolves, because a tell is only
  informative relative to how often everyone else does the same thing.
- Do NOT judge or hint at what any behavior indicates. You are surfacing candidates;
  whether a behavior actually discriminates between roles is decided later by counting,
  not by you.
- Skip table-stakes moves every player makes every day (greeting the group, "we should
  find the wolves", casting a vote per se). A candidate must be a CHOICE — something the
  player did that others in the same spot visibly did not.
- Distinct entries only: if two players exhibit the same pattern, emit one entry per
  exhibitor with the same behavior wording. If one player repeats a pattern, emit it once
  with the clearest quote.

Emit 3-8 tells for a typical day; fewer is fine if the day was thin. Quality over volume.
