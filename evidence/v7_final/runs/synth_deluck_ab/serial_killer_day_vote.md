## Cluster 1 (4 obs)

**H — halo-synthesis:**
- (defensive/deceptive) When a strong voting consensus forms and you are not under suspicion, vote with the majority. This is the default safe move to maintain your villager cover. Treat it as an opportunity if the target is a high-value town role (like an Investigator) or a rival evil (a Wolf), as it allows you to help eliminate them with minimal personal risk.

**D — credit-aware synthesis:**
- (offensive/deceptive) If you are under low suspicion and not directly implicated in the current conflict, vote with the emerging majority to maintain cover. However, if a player is demonstrating strong analytical skills and building a case that threatens you (especially in the endgame with 3-5 players), you must pivot to offense. Lead a vote against them, attacking their *methods* (e.g., 'tunnel vision', 'sowing confusion') rather than their role claim to win over undecided players. Do not escalate minor, speculative accusations against yourself; this is a losing move that draws unnecessary heat.

## Cluster 2 (4 obs)

**H — halo-synthesis:**
- (defensive/deceptive) When a strong, near-unanimous consensus forms for an elimination, vote with the majority. Do not attempt to save the target or cast a protest vote, as this will draw unnecessary and dangerous suspicion. Use the vote to solidify your villager cover, regardless of whether the target is a rival evil or a villager.

**D — credit-aware synthesis:**
- (offensive/deceptive) When a strong village consensus forms against a player who is not you, your vote depends on the game state. If it is mid-game and you are under no suspicion, vote with the majority to eliminate a rival evil or a non-threatening villager; this preserves your cover. However, this passive strategy fails in the endgame (3-5 players) or when a specific player becomes an analytical threat. In that case, you must pivot: proactively lead a vote against your biggest threat. Justify this by framing them as 'disruptive,' 'suspiciously quiet,' or 'tunnel-visioned' to peel supporters from the old consensus.

## Cluster 3 (2 obs)

**H — halo-synthesis:**
- (defensive/deceptive) When a village consensus forms for a vote, evaluate the target's strategic value to you. If you are under suspicion alongside another evil (e.g., a wolf), vote with the majority to eliminate the wolf; use them as a shield to clear your name. If you are relatively safe and the village is targeting a town power role (e.g., Investigator) out of confusion, subtly protect them or redirect the vote. Removing an info role simplifies the board and can empower a killing role (Vigilante) who is a greater threat to you.

**D — credit-aware synthesis:**
- (offensive/deceptive) When the village begins hunting for you after the primary evil faction is gone, you must seize control of the vote. If you are not the primary suspect, identify the most competent remaining player (an analyst or unconfirmed power role) and lead the charge against them, framing them as 'disruptive' or 'confusing'. If you are under direct suspicion from a specific player, vote directly for your accuser to frame the vote as a one-on-one credibility contest. Do not be passive; proactively creating a target prevents the village from consolidating against you.
- (defensive/deceptive) When you are under moderate suspicion but a stronger consensus is forming against another player, you must use that player as a shield. Join the majority and vote decisively to eliminate them. Justify your vote by pointing to public evidence, like a voting record, to frame yourself as a logical team player and divert all lingering suspicion onto the scapegoat.
